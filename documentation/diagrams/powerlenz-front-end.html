PowerLenz Front End

Version 2, after the datasheet review. From the incoming 230 V line to the ESP32-S3: where the CT and the ZMPT107-1 sit, what scales each signal, where the ADC reads it, and which three parts carry the isolation. Values are the finalized ones; anything still unproven is listed at the bottom.

Wi-Fi transmit bursts pulling current from the supplyrepeat at low rates, tens to thousands of times a secondNo. Too slow. Fix with the separate LP5907 supply for the ADC and careful layout.

These ranges are typical and not measured on our hardware. Check the real noise on the first board with an oscilloscope.

How the numbers are calculated

The ADC only ever reads a voltage. Both channels carry a scaled-down voltage, and the firmware undoes the scaling to get volts and amps back. Values come from the datasheets we read (ADS131M04, SCT-013-000, ZMPT107-1, HLK-5M05) unless marked as an estimate. Real units will differ, so every gain and phase below is calibrated on the first board.

1. ADC code to volts

V_adc = code × Vref / ( G × 2^23 )

one count (LSB) = 1.2 V / 8,388,608 = 143 nV         (G = 1)
                                       71.5 nV        (G = 2)

2. Current channel: volts to amps

I_sec  = V_adc / Rb                      (Ohm's law across the burden)
i(t)   = I_sec × N × k_i                 (the CT's ratio, then calibration)

in one line:
i(t)   = code × Vref/(G·2^23) × N/Rb × k_i

Check with the example: 91.8 mV peak ÷ 12.98 Ω = 7.07 mA, and × 2000 = 14.14 A peak, which is 10 A rms.

3. Voltage channel: volts to mains volts

i_p    = v_mains / R′                    (the ZMPT107-1 passes the primary current 1 : 1)
V_adc  = i_p × Rs
v(t)   = V_adc × R′ / Rs × k_v           R′ / Rs = 117,000 / 50 = 2,340

example: 325.3 V peak ÷ 117 kΩ = 2.78 mA peak, × 50 Ω = 0.139 V peak (code ≈ 1.94 M at G = 2)

The ADC has plenty of headroom here: at G = 2 the window is ±0.6 V, and the signal uses about a quarter of it. The real limit is the ZMPT107-1: its linearity is specified from about 46 V to 276 V rms (20% to 120% of 2 mA). Brown-outs and surges fall outside that.

4. RMS, power and power factor

V_rms = sqrt( (1/M) × Σ v_k² )           M = samples in a whole number of cycles
I_rms = sqrt( (1/M) × Σ i_k² )           (160 samples per cycle at 8 kSPS)

P  = (1/M) × Σ ( v_k × i_k )             real power, watts
S  = V_rms × I_rms                       apparent power, VA
Q  = (1/M) × Σ ( v_(k−quarter) × i_k )   reactive power with sign (voltage delayed a quarter cycle)
PF = P / S

energy (kWh) = Σ ( P × Δt ) / 3,600,000      Δt in seconds

Windows are cut at voltage zero-crossings, so each one holds whole cycles even when the grid drifts from 50 Hz.

5. The filter, and why both channels match

R_total = 2 × Rf + R_source              (1 kΩ in each leg)
fc      = 1 / ( 2π × R_total × Cf )
gain    = 1 / sqrt( 1 + (f/fc)² )
phase   = − atan( f / fc )

current channel:  R_source ≈ 13 Ω    R_total = 2,013 Ω   fc = 7.91 kHz   lag at 50 Hz = 0.362°
voltage channel:  R_source =  50 Ω   R_total = 2,050 Ω   fc = 7.76 kHz   lag at 50 Hz = 0.369°

at 4.096 MHz (the ADC's modulator rate): 20 × log10(4.096 M / 7.9 k) ≈ −54 dB

fixed gain error: 2 kΩ against the ADC's 330 kΩ input impedance → about −0.6% per channel

Two 1 kΩ resistors dominate the filter, so the source resistance (13 Ω against 50 Ω) barely matters: the two lags differ by about 0.007°. The first draft used a 938 Ω divider, which made a 0.165° mismatch. The 0.6% gain term is constant, so calibration absorbs it. Its temperature drift is not yet known.

6. Phase correction

delay = φ / ( 360° × f )                 φ = phase error between the channels

CT (about 4° measured by OpenEnergyMonitor at 22 Ω): 4 / (360 × 50) = 222 µs = 1.78 samples
  → shift the current by 2 samples (250 µs) in software,
    then set the ADC's PHASEn register for the remaining −28 µs (about −114 steps of 244 ns)

ZMPT107-1 (≤ 45 arcmin = 0.75°): 41.7 µs, about 171 steps → fits in the register alone

register range at OSR 512: −256 … +255 steps = about ±62 µs

The signs are not yet known. CTs usually lead; the ZMPT107-1's direction is unverified. The calibration finds both. The CT's 4° figure is one unit tested by one group, so ours will differ.

7. Range and resolution of the current channel

largest wire current, peak = (Vref / G) / Rb × N
                           = 1.2 / 12.98 × 2000 = 185 A peak = 131 A rms     (clips above this;
                             the CT itself saturates at about 140 A)

(the first draft's 22 Ω clipped above 77 A rms)

choosing Rb:   Rb ≤ (Vref/G) / ( I_max,peak / N )
               for 100 A rms: peak 141 A → 70.7 mA → Rb ≤ 1.2 V / 70.7 mA ≈ 17 Ω; 13 Ω leaves margin

smallest step: 143 nV / 12.98 Ω × 2000 = 22 µA of wire current per count (in theory)
noise floor:   7.56 µV rms (G = 1, 8 kSPS) / 12.98 Ω × 2000 ≈ 1.2 mA of wire current per sample

A 0.3 A load gives about 2 mV rms across the burden, against 7.56 µV of ADC noise, so the ADC is not the limit at low current. The CT's accuracy, phase error and layout are. Measure it on the first board.

8. Calibration, in order

  1. Voltage gain k_v. Compare V_rms with a reference meter, and set k_v = V_ref ÷ V_measured. This also absorbs the 0.6% filter term.
  2. Current gain k_i. Use a resistive load (a heater or incandescent lamp) and set k_i = I_ref ÷ I_measured. Repeat at a few currents, because a CT is not perfectly linear.
  3. Sign. On the same resistive load, real power must be positive. If it isn't, flip the sign of that channel in firmware.
  4. Phase φ. On the resistive load, Q must be 0. Adjust the whole-sample shift and the PHASEn register until it is.
  5. Check. Test a fan or motor and a switch-mode supply against the reference. If P and PF agree, the calibration holds across load types. Repeat from about 0.1 A upward to find the CT's low-current limit.

Suggested pins are not final. Avoid IO35–37 (the DevKitC guide says they are not available on octal-PSRAM boards), the strapping pins IO0, IO3, IO45, IO46 (from the WROOM-1 datasheet), and IO19/20 (USB D− and D+). IO10–13 are the FSPI (SPI2) pins in the DevKitC header table. IO4, IO5, IO6 are free general pins there; the choice is still ours.

Still open

  • Bench tests. Our CT's and ZMPT107-1's real phase and ratio error; CT accuracy from about 0.1 A; the jitter on the ESP32-made CLKIN.
  • CT protection. A CT secondary must never run open. The split burden keeps it loaded. TI's clamp above covers the ADC pins, but a clamp across the CT secondary itself is still to be designed.
  • HLK-5M05. The datasheet states 3000 V isolation in one place and 2500 V in another, and its circuit drawing shows a 0.5 A fuse where its text says 1 A. Its derating graphs were read: full load from about 110 V to 260 V AC and up to 60 °C. Do not connect a laptop to the board while it is on mains until the isolation rating is resolved.
  • Fuse. The Littelfuse 0239 glass fuse is only rated 35 A at 250 V AC (official table), so it is replaced by a ceramic Bel 5HT 1-R listed at 1.5 kA. That figure comes from a seller listing; read Bel's datasheet before building.
  • Input protection. TI's SBAA506 clamp is adopted on all four ADC input pins: signal, 1 kΩ, clamp node (BAT54 to AVDD, BAV199 pair to ground), 100 Ω, ADC pin, with the 10 nF C0G across each pair. It is not drawn on the schematic above. It moves the filter corner to about 7.2 kHz and the lag to about 0.40° on both channels, so the two still cancel. A TVS or Zener on AVDD is still to be chosen.
  • ZMPT107-1. Surge withstand of the primary is not stated, and its linearity is only specified from about 46 V to 276 V rms.
  • ADS131M04. Use continuous-conversion mode for AC (global-chop is for DC). Phase calibration shifts the DRDY timing, so read that section before writing the interrupt handler. The 330 kΩ input impedance's temperature drift is unknown.
  • Power. The DevKitC guide calls its power sources "mutually exclusive", so never connect USB and the HLK 5 V at the same time. A custom board would replace its regulator with an AP63203 buck.
  • Mains safety. Use an enclosure, correct creepage and clearance, and have the board reviewed by someone with mains-design experience before it is powered. Certification is our job, not the module's.
